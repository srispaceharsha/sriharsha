"Recent contribution to the mathematical theory of communication" (1949)-- Warren Weaver. [ https://www.jstor.org/stable/42581364 ] Reading notes from chapter 1: Imagine you are on a trail, hiking with few of your friends. You want to get to the summit before the Sun rises. Your enthusiastic friends are about 100 meters ahead of you, but only you know the trail path towards the summit. You see that the friends ahead are at a fork on the path and they need to turn left. Given the high windy conditions, your friends may not hear you shouting the directions at them. You can see them looking at your direction for guidance. Now you need to perhaps do hand gestures to communicate the accurate information across. You need them to take the left and continue as fast as they can, because Sun is about rise soon. "The hand gestures" -> The symbols "Turn left" -> The information "Technical problem" of communication -> Your friends may not see your gestures if you are not dramatic enough, given the distance. OR the morning mist might have reduced the visibility. Or the bushes, and plants around you might obscure the view. The symbols used to represent the message may not be transmitted accurately. "Semantic Problem" of communication -> You might have done the correct gestures, and it is clearly visible and your friends can actually see it well. But, they may not understand it correctly. They may just wave back at you, thinking you were just saying 'hi!'. The interpretation of the the information sent may not be correct. "Effectiveness problem" -> You have waved correctly, your friends understood the gestures correctly as "turn left". Yet, they may not turn left. They may just sit around until you come closer enough to confirm the instructions again. Even if the symbols are correctly sent, and interpreted -- it may not lead to intended action. In reality, all three of the problems may co-exist and create a lot of problems.
