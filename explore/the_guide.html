Notes on "The Guide" by R.K. Narayan. --- Chapter One: -- Words of interest: - Loquacious - Obstinately - Brusquely - Interminable - Stave - Pontifically - Obeisance - Prostration Thoughts on chapter one: -- ❀ Raju has suspense built into his character but not held too tightly. Just enough information is given away at the right moments, pushing the story both backward and forward. I hadn't noticed this on my first reading. ❀ Velan volunteers information freely. He is very helpful in moving the story along! ❀ The Barber is an absolutely believable character. I have seen similar one in life as such. A short-lived character but very strong one. But why did he say "no" the guessing game of how long Raju has been in the jail? That part is not clear. Is the barber’s role simply to let the audience know how long Raju was in jail? Or is it to show that Raju doesn’t speak freely about his time in prison? Or does it hint that Raju hasn’t yet decided what to do after his release? ❀ Raju comes across as a kind person who acts on impulse. ❀ End of Chapter One: It closes with a small cliffhanger, a sliver of suspense that carries into the next chapter. There’s an obvious theme of Raju speaking in weasel words, letting the listener interpret things however they want. Overall impression: So much information presented in such a simple manner! I know that Raju has been a guide, he has been to prison, there is a Rosie person who seems to be of interest, yet nothing more than a few details are revealed. Raju seems be in an ongoing evolution of his current version and his habit of poking his nose in other's business is indeed keeping him busy. --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- --- Chapter Two: -- Words of interest: - Contrite - Pyol - Bleated - Edification - Prudence - Rattan Cane Thoughts on chapter Two: -- ❀ The cow-boy character's role to push the story toward Raju's schooling years. How clever! ❀ The teacher character is stereotypical, but with a hint of humor in it. Author uses few lines to draw a large canvas, allowing the reader to fill in the necessary details to the story.
